# Intel Ohio One: research notes (November 18, 2026)

For the closing beat of `west-virginia-ascend`. **[V]** = wording checked
against the source text (SEC EDGAR, Intel PDFs). **[S]** = quoted from a page
by a fetch tool, full page not seen; re-check against the live page before
publishing.

## What the plant is designed to make

- Intel release, January 21, 2022 [V]: "two new leading-edge chip factories in
  Ohio ... powering a new generation of innovative products from Intel and
  serving the needs of foundry customers." Designed for "Intel 18A ... from
  high-performance mobile to artificial intelligence." "Production is expected
  to come online in 2025." "The scope and pace of Intel's expansion in Ohio,
  however, will depend heavily on funding from the CHIPS Act."
  https://d1io3yog0oux5.cloudfront.net/_30f8d843824dfbccc3bd0b0cd772d41f/intel/news/2022-01-21_Intel_Announces_Next_US_Site_with_Landmark_1521.pdf
- NIST CHIPS award page (undated) [S]: "Increased leading-edge semiconductor
  capacity for production of Intel 14A and future nodes"; $1.5 billion direct
  funding for Ohio; 1,500 manufacturing jobs. https://www.nist.gov/chips/intel-corporation-ohio-new-albany
- No primary source ties Ohio to a product category (PC, server, AI chips).
  18A is now made in Arizona and Oregon.

## What Intel says the timing depends on

- February 2, 2024 [S]: "We will not meet the aggressive 2025 production goal."
  https://www.ideastream.org/2024-02-02/intel-announces-delay-to-opening-of-new-albany-plant-by-more-than-a-year
- February 28, 2025 memo, via Construction Dive March 4, 2025 [S]: "continue
  construction at a slower pace, while maintaining the flexibility to
  accelerate work and the start of operations if customer demand warrants."
  Mod 1 operations 2030-2031, Mod 2 2032.
  https://www.constructiondive.com/news/intel-delays-new-albany-ohio-chip-manufacturing-project-again-2030-2031/741384/
- Lip-Bu Tan, July 24, 2025 [S]: "further slowing construction in Ohio to
  ensure our spending is aligned with demand"; "the company invested too much,
  too soon – without adequate demand"; "our investment in Intel 14A will be
  based on confirmed customer commitments."
  https://www.intel.com/content/www/us/en/newsroom/news/corporate/lip-bu-tan-steps-in-the-right-direction.html
- FY2025 10-K, filed January 23, 2026 [V]: "If we are unable to secure a
  significant external customer for our Intel 14A node, we may pause or
  discontinue development of Intel 14A and subsequent next generation
  leading-edge nodes. ... For example, we would likely discontinue the new
  leading-edge fabrication facilities we are building in Ohio." "We have been
  unsuccessful to date in securing any significant external foundry customers
  for any of our nodes." "we have delayed or cancelled manufacturing facility
  construction or expansion projects in Ohio, Germany, Poland, Malaysia and
  Israel." https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/50863/000005086326000011/intc-20251227.htm
- 2026 10-Qs [V]: Q1 and Q2 say "investing in a new leading-edge manufacturing
  facility in Ohio in the long term"; Q3 (October 30, 2026) drops "in the long
  term." All adopt the 10-K risk factors by reference. Q3: "During Q2 2026, we
  committed to completing development of Intel 14A ... the scale and pace of our
  manufacturing expansion projects will ultimately be dictated by the amount of
  committed demand for Intel 14A."
  https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/50863/000005086326000191/intc-20260926.htm
- Intel's February 2026 report to Ohio, via NBC4 March 8, 2026 [S]: no further
  delay planned; about $1.4 billion spent in 2025, more than $5 billion total;
  Fab 1 "as early as 2030"; 162 Ohio One employees.
  https://finance.yahoo.com/news/intel-spent-1-4-billion-103000156.html
- WOSU, August 26, 2026 [S]: first fab "by 2031"; deadlines "flexible based on
  customer demand" (WOSU's paraphrase).

## AI demand

- Intel ties Ohio to winning 14A customers, not directly to AI. Q3 2026 10-Q
  [V]: "demand for our data center products remained strong amid continued
  investments in AI infrastructure." Intel Foundry operating loss $1.853
  billion in Q3 2026 [V].
- John Vinh, KeyBanc, in WOSU August 26, 2026 [S]: demand drivers are "the
  demand for AI semiconductor chips" and "geopolitical diversification."
- Malcolm Penn, Future Horizons, eeNews Europe January 26, 2026 [S]: "If the
  AI bubble bursts the excess capacity at the leading edge is really going to
  hurt." About the industry and TSMC, not Intel or Ohio.
  https://www.eenewseurope.com/en/warning-signs-for-semiconductor-market-in-ai-boom/
- No named analyst found tying an AI slowdown to Ohio specifically.

## CHIPS Act and customers

- August 2025 [V, 10-K]: the U.S. government took 275 million shares; the
  CHIPS agreement was amended "removing the prior project milestone
  requirements," and Intel received the remaining $5.7 billion.
- Ohio $600 million onshoring grant, 2028 deadline (WOSU, February 28, 2025)
  [secondary, contract not checked].
- No customer confirmed for Ohio. SK hynix (Reuters, September 16, 2026, via
  TrendForce) in early talks to lease part of the site for memory; SK hynix
  says "No plans have been confirmed." https://news.skhynix.com/en/fact-10/
