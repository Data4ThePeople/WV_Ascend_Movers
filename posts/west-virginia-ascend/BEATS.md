## 2. West Virginia has been gaining people from other states

- Chart 1, `charts/01-wv-net-domestic-migration.png`. Net domestic migration
  was negative every year ending July 2013 through July 2020 (low point
  -10,139 in 2017), then positive every year from 2021 (+6,408 in 2025).
  2021-2025 total +17,349; 2015-2019 total -39,847.
- Chart 3, `charts/03-state-migration-turn.png`. West Virginia's turn ranks
  3rd of 51 (+6.34 per 1,000 residents a year). Chart shows West Virginia and
  its five neighbors only; Kentucky is the closest at +1.87 (22nd). Ohio ranks 31st (+1.11).

## 3. The Eastern Panhandle drove most of it

- Chart 4, `charts/04-ascend-counties-vs-rest.png`. Per 1,000 residents a year,
  2015-2019 to 2021-2025: Eastern Panhandle +9.99 to +20.57, other Ascend
  areas -7.08 to -1.37, rest of West Virginia -5.60 to -0.20.
- Counts for the sentence before the chart: the Panhandle gained 21,680 on net
  in 2021-2025, more than the state's 17,349. Without the Panhandle, the rest
  of the state lost 4,331.
- Cautions: county figures include moves between West Virginia counties. The
  Panhandle was already gaining before Ascend.

## 4. Ascend helps, but it is small

- Chart 5, `charts/05-ascend-reported-counts.png`. 827 members, 1,641 new
  residents including family, 184 finished the two years (September 30, 2026).
  827 is 82.7% of the 1,000-member goal set in September 2021.
- Size check: even counting every resident Ascend reports, the program equals
  about 0.4 per 1,000 a year in the Panhandle (change there +10.59) and about
  0.5 in its other areas (change +5.72; non-Ascend counties +5.41). The
  Panhandle's change beyond the non-Ascend counties is about 5,460 people; Ascend reports 421 residents there.
- Say: Ascend's members are too few to explain the migration change in its
  areas. Do not estimate what would have happened without it.

## 5. Heart: the comparison

- Transition (Eric): not the main driver does not mean not valuable.
- Ascend: $12,000 cash per member, paid monthly over 24 months (members who
  buy a home can take the rest as a lump sum). Members prove
  "employment and income prior to acceptance" (paystub or tax documents), and
  employers confirm the job. Ascend publishes average salaries by community,
  $82,122 (Charleston) to $110,020 (Eastern Panhandle); $96,052 weighted by
  members, about 70% above West Virginia's average wage of $56,470 (BLS, May
  2025).
- JobsOhio: $16,182 in grants per committed job, 2021-2025, and $66,689 in
  committed payroll per committed job.
- Language for the contrast:
  - "Ohio publishes the pay it was promised for jobs that don't exist yet.
    Ascend publishes the pay of jobs its members already have."
  - "A JobsOhio job is a promise. An Ascend member brings a job with them."
- Link the November 17 grants tool post for "we can only see when it fails":
  https://www.data4thepeople.com/p/jobsohio-grants ("There is no success
  column"; it "cannot see a project that was never built").
- Required caveats: the $12,000 is Ascend's cash incentive only; total program
  cost per member is not published. Both programs pay some people or firms who
  would have come anyway; neither measures how many.

## 6. Close: honest assessment (Eric)

- Value is in the quality and auditability of the data.
- Risk spread across hundreds of individual workers versus large single
  grants. Documented examples:
  - Petmin USA: $10 million in grants committed February 2020 for 110 jobs at
    an Ashtabula pig iron plant, all of it paid (FY2020-21). Plans for the
    plant "appear to be dead" (Star Beacon); Chapter 7 filed November 3, 2025
    (court docket).
  - Belmont County petrochemical plant: $48,599,868 paid to three companies,
    $20,000,000 returned, plant never built, $28,599,868 out (grants tool post).
  - Intel Ohio One: $125.4 million paid by FY2025 for 3,000 committed jobs.
    What it is for: leading-edge logic chips, now Intel's 14A process ("Intel
    14A and future nodes," NIST CHIPS award page); announced in January 2022
    for Intel's own products and outside foundry customers, production
    "expected to come online in 2025." First fab now due 2030-31.
    The risk in Intel's own words (FY2025 10-K, filed January 23, 2026,
    verified against EDGAR): "If we are unable to secure a significant external
    customer for our Intel 14A node, we may pause or discontinue development of
    Intel 14A ... For example, we would likely discontinue the new leading-edge
    fabrication facilities we are building in Ohio." And: "We have been
    unsuccessful to date in securing any significant external foundry customers
    for any of our nodes." Its Q3 2026 10-Q says the pace "will ultimately be
    dictated by the amount of committed demand for Intel 14A." No customer is
    confirmed for Ohio. Use these filings rather than our own view on AI
    demand; the one named "AI bubble" quote found (Malcolm Penn, Future
    Horizons) is about the industry, not Intel or Ohio. Full notes in
    `docs/intel_research.md`.
  - Ohio's whole Intel package: about $2.1 billion committed as itemized by
    the state in January 2022 (grant, infrastructure, tax credit, JobsOhio);
    $839.4 million paid through June 30, 2025 in records that name Intel
    ($600M onshoring grant, $125.4M JobsOhio, $114M water plant). The grant
    agreement's deadline for 3,000 jobs is December 31, 2028; Intel's
    February 2025 timeline puts Fab 1 operations in 2030-2031. Policy Matters
    (July 2023): the agreement doesn't cover "about $400 million Ohio is
    spending on roads and other infrastructure," nor the $300 million water
    plant. The state may waive repayment
    for "market conditions." Call out JobsOhio's $125.4M as its own line.
  - Ultium Cells does not fit "grants vanishing into no jobs": the plant is
    open, the state layoff notice lists 484 permanent and 850 temporary
    layoffs (1,334), and Business Journal Daily reported recalls (August 27,
    2026).
