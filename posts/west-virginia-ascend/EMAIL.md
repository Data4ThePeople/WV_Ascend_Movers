## 3. Body, part one

```
Over the last two days we followed the money on JobsOhio. We first built a public tool to track its grants, understand who gets the money, and what happened to it. We then followed this up by studying where the money went.

One of the main takeaways is that JobsOhio has given out a boatload of grants, but when you look at the dollars, the program has given a disproportionate amount of money to large corporations who are promising to create loads of jobs in one shot.

There is a term for this strategy in sales. It's called whale hunting.

For example, Ohio has made one huge bet on Intel, a package reported at about $2.1 billion. Ohio has already paid out at least $839.4 million of it, $125.4 million of that from JobsOhio. Whether this bet pays off rests on whether Intel can land "a significant external customer" for its new 14A chipmaking process. If it can't, Intel says it "would likely discontinue the new leading-edge fabrication facilities we are building in Ohio."

But there is an entire spectrum of other ways to approach economic development, and one of the most interesting is right next door. Instead of trying to hunt whales, West Virginia's Ascend program goes after individual workers. It pays them to move to the state and bring their very healthy income with them.
```

## 5. Body, part two

```
Ohio publishes the pay it was promised for jobs that don't exist yet (and maybe never will). Ascend publishes the pay of jobs its members already have.

By its own count, Ascend has brought 1,641 people to West Virginia since 2021: 827 workers with real jobs, plus their families. Over the same years, West Virginia went from losing people to other states to gaining them, a turnaround that ranks third among the 50 states and D.C.

But Ascend is not the main reason for that turnaround. It is still too small to explain one of the largest swings in the country. The full post follows the tax records to find out who did move, and explains why we think Ascend's real value lies somewhere else: its certainty and measurability.
```
