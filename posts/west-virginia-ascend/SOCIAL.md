Over the last two days we followed the money on JobsOhio. Much of it goes to large companies promising loads of jobs in one shot. In sales, that is called whale hunting.

Right next door, West Virginia's Ascend program does the opposite. It goes after individual workers. To join, a worker shows proof of employment and income, and the employer confirms the job. The job exists before the state commits a dollar.

By its own count, Ascend has brought 1,641 people to West Virginia since 2021: 827 workers plus their families. Members earn an average of $96,052, about 70% above the state's average wage.

Ascend is small next to the move from the Washington, D.C. area into West Virginia's Eastern Panhandle. But in our view its real value is its certainty and measurability. Ohio publishes the pay it was promised for jobs that don't exist yet. Ascend publishes the pay of jobs its members already have.
